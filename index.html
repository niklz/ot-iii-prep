1 · The role

What Grade 10 adds

Both profiles share one purpose: statutory occupational therapy and care planning for adults across B&NES, focused on physical function and equipment, to reduce the impact of ill health and disability and improve independence. The Grade 10 post adds experience, complexity and influence over other practitioners.

So the panel will look for evidence that you can

  • Reason through complex, high-risk cases and make decisions you can defend.
  • Turn a disagreement with a person, family or professional into an agreed outcome.
  • Teach, mentor and advise other practitioners and professionals.
  • Apply assistive technology with advanced, current knowledge.
  • Bring OT expertise to adaptation and housing design with surveyors, architects and housing providers.
  • Spread person-centred, strengths-based practice across the team, beyond your own cases.
  • Use resources well: the least restrictive, most sustainable way to meet need.
  • Stay calm and safe when facing conflict, hostility or distressing situations.

The same at both grades

  • OT degree or equivalent, and HCPC registration.
  • Knowledge of legislation, statutory guidance, local policy, national codes and multi-disciplinary practice.
  • Working autonomously and managing your own time.
  • No line management, though you may supervise or guide junior colleagues.
  • No budget, but you must be aware of spend when planning care and support.
  • Frequent conflict and distressing situations, and long periods of concentration.

2 · The organisation

Know B&NES

Showing that you understand where the service has come from, and where it is going, sets an experienced candidate apart.

The four values

The role profiles say these values inform recruitment decisions. Have one short example ready for each.

Bold

Aspirational goals, innovative solutions, adapting to change, and taking action that improves people's lives.

Show it with a new approach you tried, such as a creative equipment or TEC solution that avoided a larger care package, or a change that cut waiting times.

Empowered

Staff and residents have the confidence to find solutions. Staff are knowledgeable, trusted to decide and able to challenge.

Show it with a person you enabled to manage for themselves, or a colleague you gave the skills and confidence to act on their own.

Supportive

Curious, collaborative and caring towards colleagues, residents and the environment. Building trusting relationships.

Show it with joint visits, peer support after a hard case, or a partnership with health, housing or the voluntary sector.

Transparent

Honest, accountable, open to criticism and honest about mistakes, with a wish to improve and keep promises.

Show it with a mistake you owned and learned from, or an honest conversation about a decision someone didn't want.

What the social care OT team does

In scope

  • Home assessments for adults 18+ with long-term disabilities, especially where the home limits what they can do.
  • Advice on different ways of doing tasks.
  • Equipment on loan from Community Stores.
  • Minor adaptations under £1,000, usually free.
  • Major adaptations through Disabled Facilities Grants.
  • OT housing reports for people who need to move for accessibility reasons.
  • Moving and handling risk assessments, and postural and seating assessments.

Sent elsewhere

  • Short-term conditions, rehabilitation and people recently discharged from hospital go to the Care Coordination Centre.
  • Equipment for work or study goes to Access to Work.
  • Walking aids go to physiotherapy via the GP.
  • Wheelchairs go to wheelchair services, or the Red Cross for short-term use.

Digital and Technology Strategy 2026 to 2028

This is the strongest local link to the Grade 10 requirement for advanced assistive technology knowledge.

  • B&NES has no formal TEC delivery model yet. People reach telecare through social care, housing and voluntary-sector signposting to several providers.
  • The plan is to build a clear core TEC offer by test and learn, using everyday technology (sensors, reminders, smart speakers, wearables) alongside traditional telecare.
  • There is always a human alternative. Digital exclusion, rural broadband, cost and confidence are named risks.
  • Staff get digital champions and short, hands-on training. Tools such as Magic Notes and Copilot are used with human oversight.
Case study to quote

In the strategy, a young woman with a learning disability and cerebral palsy worked with her OT on adaptations and technology: door openers, a fold-down counter, an automatic fridge opener, a perching stool and an app for anxiety. Her weekly care cost fell from £1,489 to £390 and her independence grew. It is a ready-made answer to "awareness of spend".

Care Act 2014: assessment, eligibility and support planning

The role is statutory. Every assessment, plan and review is a Care Act duty, and the panel will expect you to use its language fluently.

LawBoth grades

Know

  • The wellbeing principle (section 1) underpins everything. The prevention duty (section 2) is to prevent, reduce or delay needs.
  • Assessments (section 9) must be proportionate and person-centred, and must look at the person's strengths and support network. Carers have their own right to an assessment (section 10).
  • National eligibility: needs arise from an impairment or illness, the person cannot achieve two or more outcomes, and this significantly affects their wellbeing. Learn the outcomes on the pocket card.
  • Care and support plans (sections 24 and 25) must be outcome-focused and reviewed (section 27). B&NES reviewed about 70 to 74% of people annually in 2025 to 2026.
  • Community equipment and minor adaptations costing £1,000 or less, including fitting, must be provided free.
  • Statutory guidance chapters to skim: 1 wellbeing, 2 prevention, 6 assessment and eligibility, 10 care and support planning, 13 review, 14 safeguarding.
Be ready to

Explain how you decide eligibility for someone whose main difficulty is moving around their home, and how equipment changes that decision.

OT theory, models and outcome measures

Panels often ask how theory shapes your reasoning. Linking a model to a real case shows experienced clinical reasoning.

Practice

Know

  • Person-Environment-Occupation (PEO): performance comes from the fit between the three. It suits adaptations work, where you change the environment or the task as well as supporting the person.
  • Model of Human Occupation (MOHO): volition, habituation, performance capacity and environment. Useful when motivation and routines matter.
  • Canadian Model (CMOP-E) and the COPM: the person names their goals and scores performance and satisfaction, which gives you a measurable outcome for reviews.
  • Activity analysis and grading, and compensatory versus remedial approaches. Social care OT is mostly compensatory and preventative.
  • Outcome measures give you evidence of impact for reviews, audits and service improvement.

Mental Capacity Act 2005 and deprivation of liberty

Decisions about equipment, adaptations, risk and moving home often involve people whose capacity is in doubt. Contentious cases often turn on capacity and best interests.

LawUpdated 2026

Know

  • The five principles: assume capacity; take all practicable steps to support the decision; an unwise decision does not mean a lack of capacity; act in best interests; choose the least restrictive option.
  • Capacity is specific to the decision and the time. The functional test asks whether the person can understand, retain, use or weigh the information and communicate a decision. Then ask whether any inability is because of an impairment of the mind or brain. The Supreme Court (JB, 2021) said to start with the functional test.
  • Best interests: involve the person, consult family and any attorney or deputy, and weigh past and present wishes. Use an IMCA if no one appropriate can be consulted.
  • Restrictive equipment such as bed rails, lap belts and door or bed sensors can amount to restraint. It must be necessary, proportionate and recorded.
  • In June 2026 the Supreme Court (AGNI [2026] UKSC 16) overruled the Cheshire West "acid test" for deprivation of liberty. The government has issued interim guidance, and DoLS numbers are expected to fall. B&NES had about 405 people waiting for DoLS in March 2026.
  • The Liberty Protection Safeguards are still not in force. The government said it would consult on them in 2026.
Be ready to

Explain how you would assess capacity for a decision to accept a hoist or move to a ground-floor flat, and what you would record.

Safeguarding adults and self-neglect

OTs see inside people's homes and are often first to notice neglect, self-neglect or abuse. The profile warns of frequent exposure to distressing situations.

LawB&NES

Know

  • The section 42 duty applies when an adult has care and support needs, is experiencing or at risk of abuse or neglect, and cannot protect themselves because of those needs.
  • The six principles: empowerment, prevention, proportionality, protection, partnership and accountability.
  • The statutory guidance lists ten types of abuse, including self-neglect, domestic abuse, financial abuse, modern slavery and organisational abuse.
  • Making Safeguarding Personal: ask what outcome the person wants and involve them throughout.
  • B&NES multi-agency procedures and self-neglect guidance come from the BCSSP. Several local Safeguarding Adult Reviews involve self-neglect, and the partnership's annual report summarises recent ones. Its website did not load when these notes were checked, so ask the team where the current procedures live.
  • Self-neglect and hoarding need patient relationship-building, capacity assessment and multi-agency planning, including fire safety checks.
  • CQC (November 2026) found people in the safeguarding process felt safe and empowered. Partners in Care and Health is reviewing the safeguarding pathway in 2026.

Law and local

Risk: assessment, positive risk-taking and defensible decisions

Grade 10 assessments cover "situations with greater risk", and both profiles ask for evidence-based, risk-informed decisions.

PracticeGrade 10

Know

  • Identify, assess, manage, monitor and review risk, and escalate when it is beyond your remit.
  • Positive risk-taking weighs the risks of acting against the risks of not acting, including lost independence and wellbeing. Record the person's own view.
  • A defensible decision is one a reasonable practitioner would make: based on evidence, law and policy, with options considered, the person's wishes, consultation and a clear record. It is judged on process, not hindsight.
  • The role involves frequent conflict and hostility. Know de-escalation techniques and your team's lone-working procedure.
Be ready to

Talk through a decision where the person chose a risk that worried you, and how you supported it safely.

Equipment, minor adaptations and moving and handling

Equipment is the core of the job: specify, demonstrate, arrange and review. Grade 10 brings more complex moving and handling and closer attention to spend.

PracticeB&NESUpdated 2026

Know

  • B&NES always assesses for basic equipment before considering major adaptations. Most equipment is loaned from Community Stores, and minor works under £1,000 are generally free.
  • Housing association tenants can often get minor adaptations from their landlord without an OT referral.
  • Moving and handling: carer risk assessments, hoists and slings, transfer aids and in-bed repositioning systems.
  • LOLER: equipment for lifting people, including hoists and slings, needs a thorough examination at least every six months. PUWER covers suitability, maintenance and training.
  • In September 2026 the MHRA issued a National Patient Safety Alert on patient hoists and slings (NatPSA/2026/005/MHRA) after 22 deaths were reported between 2015 and 2025. By September 2027, organisations, including community equipment services and home care, must have standard pre-use checks with a pause once the sling takes the weight, a register of compatible hoist and sling combinations, up-to-date LOLER examinations and role-specific training.
  • Single-handed care reviews: a 2022 survey found English councils run thousands of reviews of double-handed care each year, usually led by OTs, with little evidence on outcomes for the person. Put safety, dignity, preferences and carer training first. Savings should follow good outcomes.
  • Review equipment regularly: is it used, safe and still meeting the need? Return what is no longer needed so it can be reused.

Explain how you would review a double-handed care package, and what would make you keep two carers.

Major adaptations, DFGs and housing design

Grade 10 adds working with housing providers and construction professionals on design. Expect a question about a complex adaptation or a new-build scheme.

PracticeGrade 10B&NES

Know

  • The DFG is a mandatory, means-tested grant under the Housing Grants, Construction and Regeneration Act 1996, up to £30,000 in England. Councils can go further through a local housing assistance policy.
  • Two tests: works must be necessary and appropriate (the OT's recommendation) and reasonable and practicable (the housing authority's view of the property). B&NES is a unitary council, so both sit in-house.
  • B&NES route: OT assessment, referral to Housing Services if the criteria are met, and complex cases to We Care Home Improvements. Extensions are funded only in exceptional circumstances.
  • Consider every option: equipment, minor works, moving a bedroom downstairs, rehousing with an OT housing report, and future needs for progressive conditions.
  • Reading plans: door clear widths, turning space, level-access showers, hoist routes and ceiling strength, ramp gradients, and wheelchair storage and charging.
  • New homes: Approved Document M has three categories: M4(1) visitable, M4(2) accessible and adaptable, and M4(3) wheelchair user (adaptable or accessible). B&NES planning policy H7 requires a share of new homes to meet M4(2) and M4(3).
  • RCOT's Adaptations without delay matches the response to the complexity of the job, so not every adaptation needs an OT.

Grade 10

Assistive technology and technology enabled care

Grade 10 asks for advanced knowledge of assistive technology and for encouraging its use. B&NES has a new digital strategy with TEC at its centre.

PracticeGrade 10B&NES

Know

  • The range: pendant alarms, falls detectors, smoke, flood and temperature sensors, door and bed sensors, activity monitoring, medication dispensers, video doorbells, smart speakers, environmental controls, communication aids and apps.
  • Start from the outcome and the risk, then choose the technology. Consider consent and capacity, privacy, routines, who responds to an alert, and cost.
  • The B&NES strategy plans a core TEC offer built by test and learn, everyday technology alongside telecare, and always a human alternative.
  • Analogue phone lines are being switched off by January 2027. Some analogue telecare may fail on digital lines or in a power cut, so check devices and make sure providers know who relies on them.
  • Quote the strategy's case study, where OT-led technology and adaptations cut weekly care costs from £1,489 to £390.

Grade 10

Managing a complex and contentious caseload

Grade 10 explicitly includes contentious cases and handling challenge from service users, families and other professionals to agree the right outcome.

PracticeGrade 10

Know

  • Prioritise by risk: safety and falls, carer breakdown, end of life, safeguarding and hospital discharge. Keep in touch with people who are waiting, and re-triage when things change.
  • Handling challenge: listen, acknowledge feelings, explain your reasoning plainly, offer options, put decisions in writing, and know when to seek a second opinion or escalate. Treat the complaints procedure as a normal route, not a threat.
  • Professional disagreements: share evidence, use joint visits and MDT meetings, and follow the escalation or resolution policy.
  • Assertive communication is clear, respectful and firm about what the law and the evidence support.
  • Look after yourself with supervision and debriefs after distressing visits (HCPC standard of proficiency 3).

Describe a case where a family or professional disagreed with you and how you reached an agreed outcome.

Training, mentoring and supervision

Experience of training or mentoring other practitioners is a Grade 10 requirement, and both grades may supervise or guide junior colleagues.

LeadershipGrade 10

Know

  • Who you might teach: junior OTs, OT assistants, social workers, care staff and family carers, housing staff and students on placement.
  • Plan, deliver, evaluate: find the learning need, choose a method (shadowing, joint visits, demonstrations, short sessions), check understanding and follow up on practice.
  • RCOT's Career Development Framework has four pillars (professional practice; facilitation of learning; leadership; evidence, research and development) across nine career levels. Map your examples to the second and third pillars.
  • Good supervision is reflective, restorative and developmental as well as case management. Know one reflective model, such as Gibbs or Kolb.
  • B&NES staff said they prefer short, hands-on, scenario-based training, which is a useful steer for any training example.

Grade 10

Records, governance and professional standards

Both profiles require accurate, auditable records. Grade 10 complex case files may be scrutinised in complaints, safeguarding reviews or court.

StandardsBoth grades

Know

  • Good records are timely, factual and person-centred, use the person's own words, and show your reasoning: options considered and rejected, risks, capacity, consent and outcomes.
  • UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018: a lawful basis, the minimum necessary, secure sharing and the Caldicott principles. People can ask to see their records.
  • HCPC standards of proficiency for OTs (revised September 2023) put more weight on service-user-centred care, equality and inclusion, leadership, digital skills and registrants' own wellbeing.
  • HCPC standards of conduct, performance and ethics (revised September 2024) include the professional duty of candour in standard 8: be open when things go wrong.
  • RCOT published a revised edition of its professional standards in July 2026. Its Keeping records guidance is in the RCOT members' area.
  • B&NES quality assurance uses case audits (83% of 39 people gave positive feedback), with strengths-based conversation and safeguarding audit tools in development.

  • Personal dignity and respect
  • Physical and mental health and emotional wellbeing
  • Protection from abuse and neglect
  • Control over day-to-day life
  • Work, education, training or recreation
  • Social and economic wellbeing
  • Domestic, family and personal relationships
  • Suitability of living accommodation
  • Contribution to society

Eligibility: three conditions

Eligibility Criteria Regulations 2015

  1. Needs arise from a physical or mental impairment or illness.
  2. As a result, the person cannot achieve two or more outcomes.
  3. As a consequence, there is or is likely to be a significant impact on wellbeing.

Unable to achieve means not without help, or only with significant pain, distress or anxiety, or by endangering health or safety, or by taking significantly longer than normal.

Eligibility: the outcomes

Eligibility Criteria Regulations 2015

  • Managing and maintaining nutrition
  • Maintaining personal hygiene
  • Managing toilet needs
  • Being appropriately clothed
  • Using the home safely
  • Maintaining a habitable home
  • Family and other personal relationships
  • Work, training, education or volunteering
  • Using community facilities, services and transport
  • Caring responsibilities for a child

Prevent, reduce, delay

Care Act 2014, section 2

  • Prevent needs arising, for example with information, advice and falls prevention.
  • Reduce existing needs, for example with equipment, minor adaptations and reablement.
  • Delay deterioration, for example with TEC, adaptations and carer support.

Mental capacity

Mental Capacity Act 2005, sections 1 to 3

  1. Assume capacity.
  2. Take all practicable steps to support the decision.
  3. An unwise decision is not proof of incapacity.
  4. Act in the person's best interests.
  5. Choose the least restrictive option.

Functional test: can the person understand, retain, use or weigh the information, and communicate a decision?

Safeguarding

Care Act 2014, section 42, and statutory guidance chapter 14

Section 42 applies when the adult has care and support needs, is experiencing or at risk of abuse or neglect, and cannot protect themselves because of those needs.

Six principles: empowerment, prevention, proportionality, protection, partnership, accountability.

Making Safeguarding Personal: ask what outcome the person wants.

Disabled Facilities Grant

Housing Grants, Construction and Regeneration Act 1996

  • Mandatory grant, up to £30,000 in England.
  • Means-tested for adults.
  • Necessary and appropriate: the OT's recommendation.
  • Reasonable and practicable: the housing authority's view of the property.

Free under £1,000

Charging Regulations 2014, regulation 3

Community equipment (aids and minor adaptations) must be provided free. A minor adaptation is one costing £1,000 or less, including fitting.

New homes

Approved Document M, volume 1

  • M4(1) Visitable dwellings, the mandatory baseline
  • M4(2) Accessible and adaptable dwellings
  • M4(3)(2)(a) Wheelchair adaptable
  • M4(3)(2)(b) Wheelchair accessible

M4(2) and M4(3) apply when a planning condition requires them, as B&NES policy H7 does for a share of new homes.

Discharge to Assess pathways

Hospital discharge and community support guidance

  • 0 Home with no new or extra support
  • 1 Home with new or extra support, such as reablement
  • 2 Short-term bed-based rehabilitation
  • 3 A care home, usually where long-term care is likely

CQC local authority assessment

Four themes, nine quality statements

  • Working with people: assessing needs; supporting people to live healthier lives; equity in experience and outcomes
  • Providing support: care provision, integration and continuity; partnerships and communities
  • Ensuring safety: safe pathways, systems and transitions; safeguarding
  • Leadership: governance, management and sustainability; learning, improvement and innovation

Lifting equipment checks

LOLER 1998 and PUWER 1998

  • Hoists and slings used for people need a thorough examination at least every six months.
  • Equipment must be suitable for the task, maintained, and used by trained people.
  • Check sling type and size for each person, and the safe working load.
  • MHRA alert (September 2026): pause and check every attachment once the sling takes the weight, and use only compatible hoist and sling combinations.

Build your examples

Answer with STAR, then reflect

  1. SSituation. One or two sentences of context: who, where and why it was complex.
  2. TTask. What you had to achieve and why it was your responsibility.
  3. AAction. Most of your answer. What you did and why, citing the law, evidence or model you used. Say "I", not "we".

6 · Practice questions

Rehearse out loud

Pick a random question, start the two-minute timer and answer out loud using one of your examples. Then check the hints for what the panel is listening for.

Why do you want this Grade 10 role, and why B&NES?

2:00

Motivation and values

Why do you want this Grade 10 role, and why B&NES?
Specific knowledge of B&NES: social care back in-house since 2024, the improvement work that led to a Good CQC rating, the OT Assessment Centre and the digital strategy. What you bring at Grade 10: complex casework, mentoring, assistive technology and housing expertise. A link to the values and to prevention.
Tell us about a time you took a bold approach to a problem.
A new or ambitious solution with a clear benefit to someone's life. How you managed the risk, who you brought with you, what you measured and what you learned.
Give an example of empowering a person or a colleague to find their own solution.
Building on the person's abilities and networks instead of doing things for them, or giving a colleague the knowledge and confidence to decide. The outcome in the person's own words if possible.
Tell us about a mistake you made at work. What did you do?
Honesty and ownership, quick action to put it right, telling the right people (duty of candour, HCPC standard 8) and what you changed afterwards. Choose a real mistake that was recoverable.
How do you support colleagues when the team is under pressure?
Practical examples: joint visits, sharing knowledge, peer support after distressing visits and noticing when someone is struggling. Balanced with managing your own caseload and boundaries.

Caseload, risk and challenge

Talk us through a complex, high-risk case. How did you assess and manage the risk?
A structured assessment, the person's views, capacity where relevant, the options considered, positive risk-taking, multi-agency input, a clear plan with review dates, escalation, and records that show your reasoning.
A family insists their father needs a stairlift and a downstairs extension. Your assessment suggests equipment and moving his bedroom downstairs would meet his needs. They are angry and threaten to complain. What do you do?
Start with what the father wants and whether he can make the decision. Listen and acknowledge their feelings. Explain your reasoning plainly: assessed need, "necessary and appropriate", and the least restrictive sustainable option. Offer options and a trial, put it in writing, offer a review or second opinion, and explain the complaints route calmly. Record everything.
You have 30 people waiting for an assessment. How do you decide who to see first?
Risk-based triage: safety and falls, carer breakdown, end of life, safeguarding and hospital discharge. Contact while people wait, interim advice or equipment, self-help such as Ask Sara, the Assessment Centre where suitable, re-triage when things change, and escalation if a risk cannot be managed.
A health professional strongly disagrees with your recommendation. How do you handle it?
Curiosity first: understand their view and evidence, then share yours. A joint visit or MDT discussion, with the person at the centre. Use the escalation or professional-disagreement policy if needed. Stay respectful and assertive.

Equipment, adaptations and technology

How would you approach adaptations for someone with a rapidly progressing condition such as MND?
Fast-tracking, planning for future needs, interim equipment, close work with Housing and the home improvement agency, alternatives such as rehousing, realistic timescales, the person's priorities and end-of-life wishes, and links with specialist nurses and wheelchair services.
How would you contribute to the design of a wheelchair-accessible home with a housing provider and architect?
Knowing M4(3) and reading plans: circulation and turning space, door widths, level access, bathroom layout, hoist routes and ceiling strength, storage and charging, and outdoor access. Involving the future occupant, site visits and sign-off at key stages.
What is your experience of assistive technology, and how would you encourage its use?
A range beyond pendant alarms, matching technology to outcomes, consent and capacity, who responds to alerts, digital exclusion and a human alternative, the January 2027 digital switchover, B&NES's test-and-learn plans, and a cost or care-hours example.
A care agency says someone needs two carers for every transfer. How would you review this?
A person-centred moving and handling assessment, the person's abilities and wishes, equipment and techniques that might make single-handed care safe, carer training, LOLER checks and the September 2026 MHRA hoist and sling alert, monitoring after any change, and clear reasons if two carers are still needed. Savings follow good outcomes and are never the only reason.

Safeguarding and capacity

On a visit you notice signs of self-neglect and possible financial abuse. What do you do?
Immediate safety first. Ask the person what they want (Making Safeguarding Personal) and consider capacity for the specific decision. Check the section 42 criteria, raise a concern through the B&NES procedures, consult your manager, record facts and direct quotes, and follow up. Self-neglect needs patient, multi-agency work.
How do you assess capacity for a decision about equipment or a move?
The five principles. Decision-specific and time-specific. Information given in an accessible way. The functional test, then the link to an impairment of the mind or brain. Best interests if needed, the least restrictive option and a clear record.

Leading and developing others

Tell us about training or mentoring you have delivered. How did you know it worked?
The learning need, a method that suits adult learners (hands-on and scenario-based), a check of understanding, follow-up, and evidence of changed practice or outcomes.
How would you help embed strengths-based, person-centred practice across the team?
Modelling it, case discussions and reflective supervision, peer audits (B&NES uses case audits and is developing a strengths-based conversation audit), sharing good examples, and co-production with people who use services.
How do you keep your practice up to date?
CPD that meets the HCPC standards, reflection, supervision, RCOT resources and specialist sections, reading, courses, and learning from audits and Safeguarding Adult Reviews. Link it to the Career Development Framework pillars.

Partnership, system and records

How would you work with the RUH and community health teams to support a safe, timely discharge?
Discharge to Assess with home as the default, clear roles (the Care Coordination Centre for rehabilitation), early information sharing, equipment in place in time, the Community Wellbeing Hub for wider support, and follow-up.
What are the biggest challenges facing adult social care OT, and how would you respond locally?
Demand and complexity, staffing and money, waiting lists, the shift to prevention and community care, the digital switchover, DFG funding, the deprivation of liberty ruling and national reform. Finish with what you would do in B&NES.
What makes a good, defensible record of a contentious decision?
Timely and factual, the person's own words, options considered and why they were rejected, risks and how they are managed, capacity and consent, who you consulted, the outcome and a review date. Written so an auditor or a court could follow your reasoning.

If there is a presentation or written task

Ask HR whether there is one. Likely subjects for this post:

  • How would you reduce OT waiting times while keeping people safe?
  • How could technology enabled care support prevention in B&NES?
  • A written case study: identify the needs and risks, then outline your assessment and support plan.

Structure it as context, evidence, options, your recommendation, risks and how you would measure success. Keep to time and use B&NES examples and figures.
